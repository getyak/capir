## User and context

- Target user: independent recruiter or boutique-search operator
- Situation: evaluating whether Talent Signal is trustworthy and useful between high-value candidate conversations
- Existing knowledge: understands relationship-led recruiting, not Talent Signal's evidence model
- Device and environment: desktop and mobile web

## Job to be done

- When: I land on the marketing site
- I want to: understand and test how one conversation changes a candidate brief
- So I can: decide whether the product will help me preserve momentum without surrendering judgment

## Primary outcome

- Primary decision or action: try the interactive brief or enter the workspace
- User success signal: can explain what changed, inspect the source, and identify that action remains recruiter-controlled
- Business success signal:
- Measurement window:

## Product truth

- Promise: Never lose a strong candidate in the gaps between conversations.
- Evidence that supports it:
- Claims that must not be made: autonomous recruiting, candidate scoring, acceptance probability, silent external action
- Real product behavior that must be shown: evidence selection changes the interpretation and next action

## Experience direction

- Mode: persuade
- Emotional target: calm confidence
- Desired three-word character: quiet, editorial, trustworthy
- Forbidden three-word character: futuristic, theatrical, autonomous
- One memorable idea: the recommendation visibly changes when its evidence changes

## Scope

- Required surfaces: marketing home
- Required viewports: desktop and mobile
- Required states: default, evidence toggled, keyboard focus, reduced motion
- Primary flow: read promise → manipulate evidence → open demo or workspace
- Minimum scope depth: 3
- Promotion threshold: 95
